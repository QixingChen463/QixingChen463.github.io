Research Experience

Undergraduate Machine Learning Researcher, BrainWalk Project January 2025-Present
University of California, San Francisco
Advisors: Peter Yiitcan Washington, PhD and Riley Bove, MD

  • Estimated clinician-rated gait impairment (Functional Gait Assessment, 0–3) from 91 single-camera walking videos of 46 people with multiple sclerosis, landing within one level of the physical therapist on nearly every video and well clear of a median-score baseline.
  • Built the end-to-end pipeline in Python, from video to prediction: MediaPipe pose extraction, feature engineering, XGBoost training, and evaluation.
  • Reduced 69 raw gait features to 55 interpretable ones with bilateral mean and asymmetry transforms, Spearman-based hierarchical clustering (|ρ| > 0.8), and clinician-guided retention.
  • Cut prediction error further by ranking features with SHAP inside each training fold and comparing top-5/10/20 subsets, so feature selection never saw held-out participants.
  • Designed nested participant-grouped cross-validation (5 outer × 3 inner folds) with bootstrap confidence intervals, so reported accuracy reflects performance on new patients rather than on repeated clips of the same person.
  • Traced the model’s weakest class, the most impaired videos, to wall contact: the physical therapist noted it in 8 of the 9 videos scored 0, and the pose features cannot see it. Delivered a SHAP feature-importance report and a data dictionary to the clinical team.
  • How the pipeline works, in more detail than a list of bullets allows.
